Plausible causes of any gap (differences between the two setups):
1. Sample size vs full dataset: we trained on a 1,399,311-row stratified random sample of the 7,845,673 rows available (1,225,709 after cleaning). The paper used about 1,191,264 train/val rows plus a separate 1,175,692-row test subset taken from other CSV files.
2. Split ratio: ours 70%/15%/15% train/val/test (stratified); paper 80/20 train/validation plus a separate test subset. Different test sets mean the numbers are not strictly paired.
3. Duplicates: we dropped 173,602 exact duplicate rows BEFORE splitting so no identical flow appears in both train and test; the paper does not mention de-duplication. Duplicates shared across splits usually inflate test accuracy.
4. Hyperparameters assumed, not taken from the paper: batch size 256, learning rate 0.001, pooling size 2, ReLU activations, EarlyStopping(patience 5) + ReduceLROnPlateau. Paper: Adam, 25 epochs. We ran 25 epochs; best val_loss at epoch 23.
5. Class imbalance: 'class_weight' (weights {'0': 18.80908014731673, '1': 0.5136544270923757}). The paper does not mention re-balancing. Weighting the ~2-3% benign class trades some accuracy for much higher benign recall / lower FPR.
6. Features: the paper states 45 inputs; our release has 46, 41 used after dropping train-constant columns ['ece_flag_number', 'cwr_flag_number', 'Telnet', 'SMTP', 'IRC'].
7. Scaling: minmax fitted on train only (paper: not stated).
8. Task: binary (paper: binary benign vs malicious) - same task.
9. Seed 42; cuDNN LSTM/conv kernels are not bit-exact between GPUs, so re-runs can differ slightly.
10. Dataset release: original 2023 release (46 features). Obtained from a public mirror of the official CIC files (train/validation/test CSVs pooled and re-split by us).
11. Metric convention: 'Precision/Recall/F1' in the paper may be weighted or attack-positive; we report both so the right one can be compared once the paper values are filled in.
