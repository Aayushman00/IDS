Plausible causes of any gap (differences between the two setups):
1. Sample size vs full dataset: we trained on a 1,400,246-row stratified random sample of the 45,019,234 rows available (977,234 after cleaning). The paper used about 1,191,264 train/val rows plus a separate 1,175,692-row test subset taken from other CSV files.
2. Split ratio: ours 70%/15%/15% train/val/test (stratified); paper 80/20 train/validation plus a separate test subset. Different test sets mean the numbers are not strictly paired.
3. Duplicates: we dropped 422,975 exact duplicate rows BEFORE splitting so no identical flow appears in both train and test; the paper does not mention de-duplication. Duplicates shared across splits usually inflate test accuracy.
4. Hyperparameters assumed, not taken from the paper: batch size 256, learning rate 0.001, pooling size 2, ReLU activations, EarlyStopping(patience 5) + ReduceLROnPlateau. Paper: Adam, 25 epochs. We ran 22 epochs; best val_loss at epoch 17 (early stopping triggered).
5. Class imbalance: 'class_weight' (weights {'0': 14.902683978911595, '1': 0.5173578758213434}). The paper does not mention re-balancing. Weighting the ~2-3% benign class trades some accuracy for much higher benign recall / lower FPR.
6. Features: the paper states 45 inputs; our release has 39, 39 used after dropping train-constant columns (none).
7. Scaling: minmax fitted on train only (paper: not stated).
8. Task: binary (paper: binary benign vs malicious) - same task.
9. Seed 42; cuDNN LSTM/conv kernels are not bit-exact between GPUs, so re-runs can differ slightly.
10. Dataset release: 2024 re-release MERGED_CSV (39 features). Obtained from a public mirror of the official CIC files (train/validation/test CSVs pooled and re-split by us).
11. Metric convention: the abstract does not say whether F1 is weighted or attack-positive (the preprint's Table IV implies weighted, since its recall equals its accuracy); we compare with our weighted F1 and report both.
12. FPR gap: the paper's 9.17% FPR (about 1 in 11 benign flows flagged) suggests no class re-weighting; our class-weighted model trades that for missed attacks instead (see outputs_nobalance/ for the unweighted run and threshold_tuning.md).
